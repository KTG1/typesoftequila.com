Types of Tequila A field guide

Five expressions. One blue agave.

Tequila is classified into five official styles after distillation. The distinction comes from what happens next: bottling, blending, resting, or aging.

Read the bottle before you pour.

Blanco

Clear and direct

Fresh from the still, with its alcohol level adjusted using water. Also called plata or silver.

Joven

Blended

Blanco combined with reposado, añejo, or extra añejo tequila. Also called oro or gold.

Reposado

Rested

Rested for at least two months in barrels or large wooden vats.

Añejo

Aged

Matured for at least one year in oak barrels no larger than 600 liters.

Extra añejo

Long-aged

Matured for at least three years, the longest-aged official class.

An independent educational field guide. Classification details follow Mexico’s official tequila standard and the Consejo Regulador del Tequila.
